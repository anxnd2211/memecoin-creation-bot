# Review: the first live batch (2026-10-29)

## What happened

On 2026-10-28 22:52–23:17 UTC the bot made its first three live launches from the launch wallet, all with
`--follow --dev-buy auto` (2.025 SOL dev buys, second on fresh posts). All three drew no buyers and were sold at 10 s
by the flow exit in one attempt: −0.003, −0.055 and −0.056 SOL, −0.11 in all. The 3-dead pause stopped the session,
and the post-mortem blamed picking: "0% drew buyers against 88% expected, not first on the post."

Execution was clean: launches landed in about 1.15 s, sells in about 1.13 s, and the costs matched the model.

## What the investigation found

1. **It was not a cold wallet.** New devs (no launch in 12+ hours of recording) were sniped as often as devs with a
   history. At 2 SOL, devs with no earlier launch were sniped *more* (65%) than devs with 10+ (39%).
   `python3 ml/coldstart.py`, sections A–E.
2. **It was not a fingerprint.** One coin had 252 failed transactions. All came from one trader's private program
   firing a guarded buy about four times a slot. Its own check said no (error 0x6f) without ever calling pump.fun: a
   momentum bot waiting for buying that never came.
3. **The posts were dead.** On the same posts in the same minutes, the launches we followed drew nothing (a 0.51 SOL
   launch), about 1 SOL (an empty launch) and 3.6 SOL. Ours drew 0.43, 0 and 0. Independent sniper bots (wallets that
   buy create slots across 5+ devs) are real and buy pump.fun-site launches too. They did not buy launches on posts
   whose first launch had failed. The follower's only bar was a 0.1 SOL trigger buy, and when we launched (14–19 s
   after each trigger), whether the trigger was selling was already on the feed.
4. **The backtest was inflated by fake demand.** 47–50% of recorded first launches were copy launches (the same
   metadata URI on several coins: one operator, many fresh wallets) or bundled (create-slot buys by wallets that only
   ever buy that dev's coins). Selling into your own wallets is not profit. With real demand only, a first launch at a
   2 SOL bag made **+0.61 to +0.68 SOL** (not +1.32) and drew buyers **33–37%** of the time (not 50%). The
   post-mortem's 88% compared our 2 SOL launches with 5 SOL launches that included bundles.
5. **At the real hit rate, the pause was wrong.** With 1 launch in 3 drawing buyers, 3 dead in a row happens by chance
   about 30% of the time. On a 2.43 SOL wallet, pausing at 3 halved a session's expected result and doubled the chance
   of ending down.
6. **Our 0-for-3 is within the normal range.** At a 33–60% hit rate, three dead in a row happens 6–30% of the time.

## What to expect now

See the runbook's [What to expect](LIVE-RUNBOOK.md#what-to-expect). In short, measured on other devs' real-demand
launches:

- A launch draws buyers about 1 time in 3.
- A dead launch costs about 0.055 SOL.
- A 10-launch session on 2.43 SOL: a mean of +2.9 to +5.8 SOL and a 2–9% chance of ending down.

That is roughly half of what was quoted before.

The closest match to a first-on-post launch from this bot is a launch that bought exactly 2.000 SOL as the first
launch on a post under 2 minutes old. On real demand those made +0.82 SOL a launch (90% +0.65 to +1.00), were
profitable 84% of the time and drew buyers 66% of the time. The exact tiers still beat nearby amounts on real demand:
2.000 SOL was sniped 55% of the time against 31–45% nearby, in both halves. Bigger tiers pay more per SOL (0.41, 0.55
and 0.60 at 2, 3 and 5 SOL), so `--dev-buy auto`'s step-up to 5 SOL stays.

The one unknown is still our own hit rate.

## The second session (2026-10-29 06:05 UTC): the seat, not the exit

The gated follower picked `$SIA`, which had drawn 1.22 SOL from others after its create slot. Ours (`$GIRL`, 19 s
later, 2.025 SOL) drew one 0.10 SOL buy and was sold at 10 s: −0.044 SOL.

On the same post, `$SIA` (the first launch, also 2.02 SOL) drew **11.22 SOL of sniper buying in its create slot**,
and then the snipers sold it down. Snipers buy the first launch on a post and skip the second. That makes 0 of 4 live
second launches with any sniper buy. The +0.74 that recorded second launches suggested does not hold for us, so in
live mode the follower now only watches (`--follow-live` overrides). Meanwhile the X watcher had caught two fresh
@Daily_MailUS posts where we could have been first, both blocked by the cooldown from `$GIRL`.

The owner asked whether we sell too fast. `npm run hold-check` replays our 2 SOL bag on 1,018 real-demand first
launches under 19 plans:

- **Holding longer made less** on every plan (−0.03 to −0.14 SOL a launch, both halves).
- **A launch nobody buys in its first 10 s** got 1+ SOL of buying later only 3% of the time, the same whether its dev
  sold within a minute or held 5+ minutes.
- **Selling sooner, into the sniper wave,** made less too (−0.09 to −0.16).
- On 10 sniped exact-2 SOL launches, selling at 3–5 s looked better, but the two halves disagreed (+0.48 / −0.03).
  That is noise, not a rule.

The exit stays.

A correction to the numbers above: launches like ours are also single-wallet (no token passing). Adding that check,
first launches made **+0.55 a launch at a 2 SOL bag** (posts ≤30 s old +0.63). Exact-2-SOL launches made +0.28 to
+0.86 depending on how strictly "first" is defined (23–50 launches), not the +0.82 quoted earlier.

## The third session (2026-10-29 06:17 UTC): the wallet's record

The X watcher found a @Daily_MailUS post 17 s old. We were the first and only launch on it (`$AMERICA`, 2.025 SOL).
It drew no sniper buy and one 0.02 SOL buyer, and was sold at 10 s: −0.055.

That was the fifth live launch in a row without a sniper buy. Our first-ever launch drew 3. So the seat was not the
whole story: the wallet's record is. On single-wallet, real-demand launches with a 1.9+ SOL dev buy:

| The dev wallet's earlier record | Sniped | Drew buyers | At a 2 SOL bag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No earlier launch | 45% | 44% | +0.68 |
| 1–2 earlier, all dead | 27% | 14% | +0.21 |
| 3+ earlier, all dead | 20% | 6% | +0.21 |
| An earlier busy launch | 53% | 53% | +1.01 |

Each dead launch lowers the next one's chances. v4 raised the pause to 6 on a simulation that treated launches as
independent, and that was wrong: v5 puts it back at 3, the rule v1 adopted to protect the record. The four follower
launches cost the wallet far more than their 0.2 SOL.

The launch wallet now shows five dead launches and no busy one: the 6%-draw group. What to do about that is the
owner's decision (see the options in the conversation of 2026-10-29).

## The cause (2026-10-29 19:57 UTC): a missing metadata field

The new launch wallet's first launch (`$REAL`, first and only launch on a 25 s old @PlanetOfMemes post, exactly
2.025 SOL) drew no sniper either: six live launches, none sniped. That is about a 3% chance at the recorded rate, so
the cause had to be something about our coins themselves.

Comparing our metadata JSON with coins the bots bought showed it. Every one of theirs carried
`"createdOn": "https://pump.fun"`, and ours did not. The bot had left the field out on purpose, so as not to look like
a pump.fun-site launch.

On a sample of 366 recorded real-demand launches of 1.9+ SOL:

| Launches hosted on ipfs.io | Sniped (each dev once) | Drew buyers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| with `createdOn` pump.fun (161) | 59% (90% 52–67%) | 66% |
| without it, like ours (17) | 10% (0–30%) | 18% |

`createdOn` names the platform, not the website: uxento and Pinata-based tools write `https://pump.fun`, j7tracker
`pump.fun`, rapidlaunch its own domain. It is true of every coin this bot makes, so the metadata now carries
`"createdOn": "https://pump.fun"` (`src/live/ipfs.ts`).

None of our live launches so far tested the strategy. Every one went out without the field. A website that is just
the X post, also checked, is fine: sniped 71% of the time.
